Sustainability Through the Ages

From lead-free chips to a net-zero future: the milestones behind Intel's commitment to a greener world.

Scroll to explore the timeline  |  Hover over a card to learn more

2007

Going Lead-Free

Intel announced it was eliminating lead from its microprocessors, starting with its 45nm Hi-k processor family, removing a toxic metal from billions of future chips.

2008

Top Green Power Buyer

The U.S. EPA recognized Intel as the largest voluntary purchaser of green power in the United States, a spot the company went on to hold for years.

2020

RISE 2030 Strategy

Intel launched its RISE strategy and 2030 goals, making sustainability a core pillar alongside being Responsible, Inclusive, and Enabling through technology.

2022

Net-Zero Pledge

Intel committed to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) across its global operations by 2040, backed by major investments in energy conservation.

2023

99% Renewable Electricity

Intel reached 99% renewable electricity across its global operations, putting its goal of 100% renewable power within reach.

2030

The Road Ahead

Next up: net-positive water, zero waste to landfill, and a tenfold boost in the energy efficiency of Intel's client and server processors, all by 2030.

Where Intel Focuses Today

Climate & Net Zero

Intel has committed to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) across its global operations by 2040, and reached 99% renewable electricity in 2023.

Frequently Asked Questions

RISE is Intel's 2030 strategy. It stands for Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable and Enabling: operating responsibly, building an inclusive culture, reducing environmental impact, and using technology to enable positive change.

Scope 1 emissions come directly from sources a company owns or controls, such as process gases and on-site fuel use. Scope 2 emissions come from the electricity, steam, heating and cooling it buys. Intel's net-zero pledge covers both.

It means restoring more fresh water to communities and watersheds than the company uses in its operations. Intel has set this as a goal for 2030.
